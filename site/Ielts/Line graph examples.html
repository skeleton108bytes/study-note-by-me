Introduction

The line graph presents data on the number of foreign visitors to the UK from 1989 to 2009, categorized into the three purposes of their visits.

Overview

Overall, all three ==reasons experienced a rise in visitor numbers.== Additionally, for the most part, holiday was the most common reason for people going to the UK, while meeting friends and relatives was the least common one.

Body

At the beginning of the period, 5 million people traveled to the UK for their business trips, which was much higher than the figure for meeting friends and relatives, which was only about 3.5 million. ==Thereafter,== the former purpose ==witnessed a decline== to around 4 million people in 1991, before markedly recovering to a peak of 8 million in 2008, finally falling by 1 million in 2009. ==An upward trend was also witnessed in== visits for meeting friends and relatives, as its ==figures underwent gradual growth==, ==increasing twofold== from 1989 to 7 million in 2008, before receding to just under 6 million people at the end of the given period.

On the other hand, ==a more volatile pattern== was ==observed in== holiday trips. the number of people traveling to the UK for this purpose started at 6.5 million, ==followed by a rise== of nearly 2.5 million people in 1195, before failing back by the same amount in 2004. Subsequently, this category saw a recovery in numbers, ==reclaiming its lead== and reaching a high of 9 million again in the final year.

Tham khảo: The line graph illustrates the fruit production, measured in millions of tonnes, in four different countries (Spain, Turkey, France, and Germany) over a 40-year period from 1970 to 2010.

Overall, while Turkey and France showed a general increase in fruit production, Spain and Germany experienced a decline, particularly in the latter part of the period. Throughout the entire period, Spain maintained its position as the leading fruit producer among the four nations.

Examining the countries with downward trends, in 1970, Spain produced approximately 5.7 million tonnes of fruit, significantly higher than Germany's output of about 2.5 million tonnes. Subsequently, Spain's production initially increased to roughly 6 million tonnes by 1980, after which it gradually declined to about 5 million tonnes by 2010. Germany, in contrast, demonstrated a more consistent decline in its fruit production, falling to approximately 1.4 million tonnes by 1990 and continuing to decrease, ending at just above 1.2 million tonnes by the end of the period.

Regarding the countries that showed growth, Turkey began with the lowest production level in 1970, at just below 2 million tonnes, while France started slightly lower at approximately 1.2 million tonnes. Turkey then demonstrated remarkable and steady growth in its fruit production, rising consistently from its initial figure to surpass 3.6 million tonnes by 2010, becoming the second-largest producer. Meanwhile, France's figures increased to about 2.2 million tonnes by 1990, then dropped to approximately 1.8 million tonnes in 2000, before climbing again to around 2.8 million tonnes in the final year.
